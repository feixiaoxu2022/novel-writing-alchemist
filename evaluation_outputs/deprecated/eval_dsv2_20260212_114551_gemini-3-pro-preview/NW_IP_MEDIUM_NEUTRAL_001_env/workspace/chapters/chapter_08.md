# Chapter 8: The Iron Beast

The air at the Madam's Tomb was thick enough to chew. It wasn't just the humidity of the impending storm; it was the silence. A heavy, suffocating silence that pressed down on the hundreds of people gathered in the cemetery.

John Marston, inhabiting the body of Zhang Mazi, stood near the front, his hand resting casually near the holster of his Cattleman Revolver. Beside him, Liuzi fidgeted, adjusting the nickel-plated revolver on his right hip—a trophy from the Pinkerton supplies—before checking the Mauser broomhandle tucked into his sash on the left. The kid looked like a walking contradiction: half cowboy, half bandit, all nerves.

"Stand still," John murmured, the words barely escaping his lips.

"I feel like a duck in a thunderstom, Dad," Liuzi whispered back, his eyes darting around the perimeter. "Something ain't right. The birds stopped singing."

At the podium—a makeshift wooden structure draped in white mourning cloth—Tang, the self-proclaimed Master and reluctant advisor, was sweating through his silk robe. He held a eulogy scroll with trembling hands. He was supposed to be praising the "bandit-suppressing heroes," but he looked like he was reading his own death warrant.

"Today..." Tang's voice cracked. He cleared his throat, the sound amplified by the unnatural quiet. "Today, we gather to honor... to honor the peace brought by Governor Huang..."

John didn't look at Tang. His eyes were scanning the ridge line opposite the cemetery. It was a high slope, commanding a perfect view of the grave site. Too perfect.

*If I were Milton,* John thought, *I wouldn't be down here in the mud. I'd be up there.*

He saw a glint of metal. Not a rifle scope. Something bigger.

"Get down!" John roared, shoving Liuzi hard.

*CRACK!*

The first bullet didn't hit a person; it shattered the flower vase on the podium, sending ceramic shrapnel slicing past Tang's ear. The Master let out a high-pitched squeal and dove behind the massive stone tombstone of the Madam, scrambling on all fours like a frightened badger.

John and Liuzi hit the dirt and rolled, sliding into the cover of the same tombstone just as the air erupted.

It wasn't the pop-pop-pop of rifles. It was a deep, rhythmic chugging, like a steam engine from hell. *Thump-thump-thump-thump-thump.*

"Mother of God," John breathed.

The tombstone shuddered violently. Stone chips sprayed over them like hail. On the ridge, the "Iron Beast" had awakened. A Maxim gun. The pinnacle of industrial death.

"What is that?!" Liuzi screamed, covering his head as chunks of granite rained down. "It sounds like a train!"

"It's a machine gun!" John yelled over the roar. "Stay down!"

Up on the ridge, Agent Milton stood with his hands clasped behind his back, watching the chaos through binoculars. He looked like a scientist observing bacteria in a petri dish. Beside him, the body double of Huang Sirang—a poor fool dressed in the Governor's finery—was trembling so hard his hat was vibrating.

"Agent Milton!" the double stammered. "My men... I'm down there! That's me down there!"

"Acceptable casualties," Milton said coldly, not lowering the binoculars. "Suppressing fire. Keep them pinned."

The gunner adjusted his aim. The stream of heavy caliber bullets walked across the cemetery, tearing up the earth, shredding banners, and turning the wooden podium into toothpicks. The crowd had scattered in panic, leaving the three men trapped behind the shrinking cover of the Madam's tombstone.

"Mr. Zhang! Mr. Zhang!" Tang was curled into a ball, his face pressed into the dirt, hands over his ears. "We're going to die! We're going to be minced meat!"

Suddenly, the firing stopped. The silence that followed was even more terrifying.

A voice boomed from the ridge. Milton was using a megaphone.

"Mr. Marston! Or should I say, Mr. Zhang!"

The English words floated down, crisp and alien in the humid air.

John gritted his teeth. He looked at Tang. "Translate."

"What?" Tang sobbed.

"Translate what he's saying!" John barked, grabbing Tang's collar.

"He... he says..." Tang listened as Milton spoke again.

*"There is no escape. The age of the outlaw is over."*

"He says... the bandits are finished!" Tang wailed. "Times have changed!"

*"I offer you a choice. Surrender now, and you will face a civilized trial."*

"He says... surrender and he'll... he'll let the law deal with you!" Tang cried out, flinching as a stray bullet from a sniper pinged off the ground nearby.

*"Refuse, and this machine will turn your legacy into dust."*

"If you don't... that iron monster will turn us all into dust!" Tang buried his face in his sleeves. "Oh, my ancestors! I don't want to be dust!"

Liuzi's eyes were wide, filled with a mixture of fear and anger. "I can take him," he growled. He popped up, raising his nickel-plated revolver, trying to fan the hammer like he'd seen John do.

"No!" John lunged.

The Maxim gun roared again instantly. A line of bullets chewed up the top of the tombstone, missing Liuzi's head by inches. John dragged the boy back down, slamming him into the mud.

"You can't fight that with a pistol, son!" John shouted. "That's a weapon of war!"

"So we just sit here and die?" Liuzi spat, wiping dust from his face. In his panic, his hand drifted to the Mauser at his waist—the bandit instinct taking over. "I'll rush 'em! I'll spray 'em!"

"You'll be dead before you take three steps!"

The tombstone was disintegrating. The heavy rounds were chipping away the edges, making their cover smaller by the second. John could feel the vibrations in his chest. It was the sound of the encroaching century, the sound of the civilization he had run from his whole life. It was here, in Goose Town, spitting fire.

John looked at the ridge. The gunner was protected by a steel shield. The distance was about two hundred yards. A difficult shot for a rifle. Impossible for a revolver.

Unless...

John closed his eyes for a second. He felt the familiar ache in his soul, the warning that this body wasn't built for what he was about to do. The last time he used it, he had slept for twelve hours. Now, he was already exhausted.

But the tombstone cracked. A large chunk fell off, exposing Tang's leg. The Master screamed.

"Five seconds!" Milton's voice echoed.

John drew his Cattleman. The weight felt reassuring, but heavy.

"Liuzi," John said, his voice strangely calm amidst the noise. "When I shoot, you run for the bamboo forest. Don't look back. Drag Tang with you."

"What about you?"

"Just run."

John took a deep breath. He didn't just focus; he *pushed*. He pushed his consciousness outward, forcing the world to bend to his will.

*Dead Eye.*

The roar of the Maxim gun deepened, slowing into a rhythmic, bass-heavy thud... thud... thud.
The flying stone chips hung in the air like suspended diamonds.
The color drained from the world, leaving everything in a sepia-toned haze, except for the bright red targets that painted themselves in John's vision.

His heart hammered against his ribs like a trapped bird. A high-pitched whine started in his ears, drilling into his brain.

*Focus.*

He stood up. To the world, he was a blur. To John, he was moving through molasses.
He saw the muzzle flash of the Maxim gun, a slow-blooming flower of fire.
He saw the gunner's head peeking just above the steel shield.
He saw the ammo belt feeder, exposed.

*Mark. Mark.*

John didn't fan the hammer. He pulled it back with deliberate, agonizing slowness.
The crosshair settled on the gunner's forehead.
Then on the ammo belt mechanism.

*Bang. Bang.*

He released the ability.

Time snapped back with the force of a physical blow. The sound of his two shots merged into one deafening crack.

On the ridge, the gunner's head snapped back, a red mist spraying the air. He collapsed onto the trigger, but the second bullet had already shattered the feed mechanism. The gun jammed with a metallic screech, followed by a dull *clunk*.

The Iron Beast fell silent.

"Go!" John roared, but the sound seemed to come from underwater.

His vision swam. The world tilted sideways. A wave of nausea hit him so hard he nearly dropped his gun. The ear-splitting ringing drowned out all other sound. He felt like his soul was being pulled out through his nose.

He felt hands grabbing him. Liuzi.

The boy wasn't running alone. He had Tang under one arm and had grabbed John with the other.

"Dad! Move!" Liuzi's voice was muffled, distant.

John stumbled, his legs feeling like rubber. He forced them to move. They scrambled away from the tombstone, dashing toward the dense green wall of the bamboo forest.

Behind them, Milton was shouting orders. Rifle fire erupted, bullets zipping past them, cutting down bamboo stalks. But the heavy pounding of the Maxim was gone.

They crashed into the undergrowth, the thick bamboo swallowing them whole.

John leaned against a stalk, gasping for air, his lungs burning. He wiped his nose and saw blood on his glove.

"We made it," Liuzi panted, his eyes shining with a mix of terror and awe. "You... you shot the machine gun. You shot the damn machine gun!"

Tang was on the ground, vomiting from fear. "The English... the English are coming..." he gibbered.

John holstered his gun, his hand trembling uncontrollably. He looked back through the leaves at the ridge. The machine was silent, but he knew it was only temporary.

"They aren't English, Master Tang," John rasped, the world slowly coming back into focus. "They're the future. And they're here to bury us."
